About This Guide

Based on the first-hand experience of students who have lived and studied in Cairo, primarily in Nasr City. Expanded and structured by Al-Maslak al-Muyassar. Your experience may vary — use this as a foundation and lean on the student community for the latest.

01

Visa (Iqāmah) Process

Understanding Egypt's visa system before you travel will save you significant stress and money. There are two stages: getting your initial entry visa at the airport, and renewing it once you are there.

Initial Visa at the Airport

Purchase your visa before passing through passport control. Cost: $30 USD cash — do not rely on airport ATMs or card machines, which are unreliable. The initial visa lasts 30 days. You will also receive an arrival form — fill it in before reaching passport control, in Arabic if possible.

E-Visa Option

Apply online before travelling to skip some queues and get a longer initial validity. Downside: Egypt's online systems can be unreliable with occasional disconnects. Most students who use it find it works, but on-arrival is simpler if you are uncertain.

Renewing Your Visa

When your 30-day visa expires you have a two-week grace period. Do not let it lapse — you risk a fine or deportation if stopped.

Critical — Always Carry Valid Documents

Never leave your accommodation without your iqāmah and passport — or at minimum a photocopy of each. A phone photo is a last resort and may not be accepted. When your iqāmah is being processed, carry the passport stamp as proof you are in renewal.

The main immigration office is Abasia. The New Cairo branch is another option. Some students use an agent who handles the process personally — based on recommendations, more tailored to your situation.

Abasia — Getting to the Right Floor

You need the 5th floor (foreigners). Take escalators to the 3rd floor, walk to the opposite side, take elevators to 5th. Going around 9am is slightly less busy.

Two Systems at the Office

Ṭarabīt (Fast Track) — Recommended
Costs an extra ~$65. They handle the process for you. Most students consider it worth it to avoid complications.
Regular System
Available on the 5th floor. Follow the steps below. Expect 2–3 hours. Iqāmah card ready roughly one week later.

Required Documents

  • Your passport
  • Cash: roughly $200 + a few hundred Egyptian pounds — cash only, no cards
  • Proof of money exchange: bank receipt no older than 1–2 weeks
  • Passport photos: three copies (they also take one at the office)
  • House contract: three copies. If you don't have one, buy a blank A4 contract from a bookshop (~10 EGP) and have a local fill it out
  • Passport photocopies: three copies of your main page
  • Entry stamp copies: three copies of the stamp on arrival
  • Your visa (e-visa or pay-on-arrival)
  • A pen, an Egyptian phone number, and an Egyptian address
Keep $200 Cash Ready at All Times

Bring US dollars from home — they are difficult to obtain in Egypt except via Western Union or connections. Do not leave this until the last minute before renewal.

Step-by-Step at the Office

  1. Arrive early with all documents organisedGo to the counter — they check everything. Any missing item means being sent back.
  2. Fill out the formName, nationality, ethnicity, passport number, place of birth, Egyptian phone number and address, purpose and length of stay, signature.
  3. Receive your queue numberDocuments placed in a folder. Wait in the main hall until called.
  4. Window reviewWhen called, go to the desk — they review your details.
  5. Pay at the bank counterThey direct you to the internal bank to pay required fees.
  6. Return with bank receiptBack to the same counter with your receipt as proof of payment.
  7. Photo booth and fingerprint scanPhotograph taken and handprint scanned.
  8. Confirm collection and get passport stampThey confirm your collection date and stamp your passport. This stamp proves you are in renewal until the card arrives.
Pickup — Choose a Mall

Select a mall for collection, not home delivery. Delivery to your address is unreliable. Citystars Mall has its collection branch on floor -5.

02

Finding Accommodation

There is no central listing site for student housing in Egypt. Almost everything is found through people. Once you are connected to the student community, options become available quickly.

When to Start Looking

Ask roughly one month before you want to move in — not too far in advance. Things change constantly and a room you are told about today may be gone before you arrive.

Areas in Cairo

Nasr City (مدينة نصر)Districts 8, 9 & 10
Recommended
The hub of the foreign Muslim student community in Cairo. Nearly every major institute is here or nearby. High concentration of other students. If a driver doesn't know your street, say "Souq As-Sayarat" — a landmark almost every Cairo driver recognises.
Gardenia City CompoundWithin Nasr City
Good for Families
A gated residential compound within Nasr City. Quieter and more organised. Popular with families and students who prefer a calmer environment while remaining within reach of the institutes.
Rehab City~20 minutes from Nasr City
Families / Comfort
A large integrated gated community east of Cairo. Green spaces, good infrastructure, safer. More expensive. Best for families rather than single students on a budget — factor in the daily commute to institutes.
New Cairo / TagammouFurther from institutes
Families · Higher Cost
Modern, westernised area further from most institutes. Significantly more expensive but better infrastructure and quieter streets. Some families prefer it. Expect a daily commute — not recommended for single students on a budget.

Two Factors Students Consistently Underestimate

Noise Pollution
Near a main road: constant traffic, beeping, dogs, disturbance — especially at night. Cheaper flats tend to be noisier. Consider your sleep and study focus before choosing purely on price.
Floor Level
Many buildings have no working lift. Being on the 8th floor means climbing stairs five times a day for ṣalāh. Students report this becoming exhausting and making them reluctant to go out.

How to Find a Flat

  1. WhatsApp and Telegram student groupsThe most effective route. Ask to be added to housing groups — rooms come up regularly and are often already informally vetted.
  2. Ask at your institute on arrivalMany institutes have notice boards or staff who know of available flats.
  3. Harunco Real Estate Egypt (Telegram)A Telegram-based service students have used for finding accommodation. Link in the Community section.
  4. Facebook groupsSearch "Cairo housing," "Muslims in Cairo," "Egypt expats." Check post dates — listings go stale quickly.
  5. Local brokers (simsār)Charge roughly one month's rent as a fee (non-refundable). Only use one pressed for time, with a recommendation. Go directly to the landlord where possible — estate agents add cost and complexity.
Never Pay Without Viewing

Never send money for a flat before physically seeing it. Scams targeting foreign students exist. If you have no connections on arrival, pre-book an Airbnb in Nasr City for a few nights and view flats in person from there.

Before You Sign Anything

  • Get a written contract — essential protection against unexpected eviction
  • Pay a deposit (~one month's rent) — photograph the condition of everything on arrival
  • Simsār fee (~one month's rent) if using an agent — non-refundable
  • Unfurnished flats can be cheaper long-term but require buying all appliances including AC and stove
  • Is electricity included? Water? Internet? Who pays if bills are not included?
  • Is there a working lift? What floor is the flat on?
  • Who to contact if something breaks?
Tap Water

Do not drink the tap water in Cairo. Buy bottled water or install a filter. This is an ongoing cost most guides don't mention — budget for it from day one.

Egypt offers a range of study options depending on your level, goals, and learning style. The main choice is between a structured markaz (institute) and private tuition — many students combine both.

Markaz Furqānمركز الفرقان
Well-Established · Highly Rated
Cost: 3,000 EGP/level (book included) — ~$60 USD Per level: 5 weeks · 2 hrs/day · 5 days/week Break between levels: ~3–4 days Books: Furqān's own curriculum · ~90 pages/level Enrollment: In person at the markaz · wait for new intake Updates: Telegram channel (intake dates, announcements)
One of the most recognised Arabic programmes among foreign students in Cairo. Uses its own well-established Furqān curriculum with a thoughtful pedagogical approach: early levels focus entirely on everyday vocabulary — the language you need to communicate in the street, run errands, and navigate daily life — before progressively introducing religious and scholarly vocabulary in later levels.

This is a meaningful differentiator. Some marākiz front-load religious terminology from the start, which is less useful for real-world communication. Furqān builds practical Arabic first, then deepens into the religious vocabulary students ultimately need.

Vocabulary is introduced gradually — no expectation to memorise 30 new words overnight. Books are deliberately short (~90 pages per level), which keeps the workload manageable and the content focused.

A student's honest assessment: "The best markaz for pushing hard in the Arabic language, especially at higher levels. No overload, vocabulary is introduced little by little. Very good for serious study."

Building exterior · Official entrance sign (Ministry of Social Solidarity registered)

One of the oldest and most well-known Arabic institutes for foreign students in Cairo, officially registered as an association with Egypt's Ministry of Social Solidarity. The programme covers 19 levels (0A through 17), totalling 950 class hours — the most comprehensive level structure of any markaz in this guide. Three campuses: Nasr City (most students), Dokki, and Ma'adi.

Fajr's strength is spoken Arabic — conversational fluency, practical vocabulary, and communication are central to the programme. Grammar is taught, but the depth of Naḥw and Ṣarf is less intensive than at Ibānah or Furqān. Those primarily targeting Classical Arabic or deep grammar study may want to supplement with a private teacher. The summer intensive programmes named after the classical scholars Sibawayhi and Ibn Aqeel signal genuine classical credentials.

The group class is currently discounted from $140 to $91/level — a significant reduction that makes Fajr more price-competitive with other marākiz. Progression is merit-based: fail the level test and you repeat it. New intakes open approximately every 30 days.

Studying Arabic Privately

Teachers typically travel to your home or choose a meetup location. Usually one-on-one, though group lessons (roommates sharing the cost) are also common and provide accountability. Always give at least 24 hours' notice to cancel — this is expected in Egyptian teaching culture.

Advantages
  • Flexible syllabus
  • Teacher focused entirely on you
  • Target specific weak areas
  • Group option adds accountability
Limitations
  • More expensive per hour
  • No formal exams or structure
  • Requires strong self-discipline
Finding a Private Teacher

Almost entirely through recommendations. Ask in WhatsApp/Telegram support groups, through roommates, at the masjid or markaz. Common texts: Bayna Yadayk, Ajrūmiyyah, Qiṣaṣ al-Nabiyyīn.

Your approach depends on your current stage — learning to read, fixing tajwīd, memorising, revising, or studying qirāʾāt. Be honest about where you are before choosing a method.

A Realistic Expectation

Following lessons in Arabic is very difficult at first. With time it becomes easier and actually assists your Arabic significantly. Be patient with yourself if you start early.

Finding Scholars and Specialised Teachers

This operates almost entirely through recommendations and word of mouth. Most respected scholars are not publicly advertised. You reach them through a connection — another student introduces you. Many have Telegram groups where they share syllabuses and schedules.

Mutūn

  • A teacher visits a student's home — open invitation or closed class for invited students
  • Lessons at the teacher's own location
  • Some marākiz offer mutūn study as part of a distinct academic progression
Serious Warning — Villages and Police

Police specifically patrol villages populated with Ahl al-Sunnah and actively look for reasons to arrest foreigners — sometimes with no legal justification beyond their presence in the area. If considering villages, seek advice from trusted brothers with experience first, and never go without full documentation. This is not a theoretical risk.

04

Living in Egypt

Cairo can feel overwhelming at first. Nearly every student goes through an adjustment period. The practical information below will help you navigate it faster.

SIM Cards & Mobile Networks

Official Outlet — Recommended
Go to Etisalat, WE, or Vodafone. They check your visa and give you a SIM for its length. Getting one at the airport on arrival is the simplest option — though it stops working when your 30-day visa expires.
Local Shops
Some local shops sell pre-activated SIMs. Ask around to find which specific shops stock these. Useful if you miss the airport option or need a longer-lasting SIM without visa tie-in.
Phone Blocking After 90 Days

A phone from outside Egypt will be blocked from Egyptian networks after 90 days. Pay a fee at a provider's office to fix it, or buy a cheap local phone to use as a Wi-Fi hotspot with your Egyptian SIM.

ATMs & Banking

  • ATMs throughout Cairo — most at bank branches. Some close when their shop does
  • Withdrawal limit: 4,000 EGP per transaction. Remove and reinsert card for another transaction
  • Always bring a backup card — if an ATM swallows your card, recovery is extremely difficult
  • Use Starling or Revolut for no-fee withdrawals. Western Union for USD. Avoid airport exchange bureaus

Transport

Uber / InDrive / DiDi
Convenient and recommended for most journeys. Uber has a motorbike option — faster but more dangerous as bikes weave through traffic. All three apps are used among students.
Tuktuks
Auto rickshaws — fit 2 comfortably. Agree the price before getting on — drivers will try to raise it afterwards. Know the landmark near your destination. Most will turn off music if asked.
Public Buses
Fast but chaotic. Pay cash on entry. You need to know the bus number, your stop, and the pickup point. Travel with an experienced student the first few times.
Navigation
Google Maps works in most areas. Bookmark key locations. Buildings are often identified by nearby landmarks — ask locals and other students for the names used on the ground.

Food & Ordering

Ṭalabāt App
Egypt's main delivery app for food, groceries, and household essentials. Used heavily by students in Nasr City — equivalent to Uber Eats.
Amazon Egypt / Noon
Amazon Egypt is affordable for household essentials. Noon is a local alternative. Both deliver reliably to Nasr City.
Direct from Restaurants
Many students save the WhatsApp number of specific restaurants and order directly — often cheaper and more convenient than apps.
Local Shops & Markets
Most local shops don't appear on maps — find them by walking around. Fresh produce and basics are cheap and readily available in student areas.

Language

Egyptians speak ʿĀmmiyyah (Egyptian dialect), not Fuṣḥā. You will rarely practice classroom Arabic in everyday public life. Near institutes, shopkeepers often know Fuṣḥā from years of student interaction. Speaking Fuṣḥā in public marks you as a foreigner, which can sometimes lead to being overcharged.

Cultural Notes

Dress: Thobes are normal and respected. Sandals are worn everywhere. A mosque hat is acceptable — a ghutrah or shemagh is not culturally normal in Egypt.

Plugs: European 2-pin standard. Bring an adapter.

Loudness: Egyptians are naturally loud in conversation — not aggression, just normal. You will adjust.

Taking initiative: Don't wait to be invited. Ask to join others — students don't always know how to invite people well. Be organised with your time; students who plan their days benefit far more from their stay.

05

Safety & Police

Most Important Rule — Always Carry Your Iqāmah

Never leave home without your iqāmah and passport — or at minimum a photocopy of each. A phone photo may not be accepted. When your iqāmah is being processed, carry the passport stamp as proof of renewal.

Police activity against foreign students is not constant, but it is real. Cases most commonly involve brothers with visible Islamic appearance (large beard, Islamic clothing) who fit a description associated with terrorism in the minds of some officials. The police to be most aware of are typically not those in marked cars — plain-clothes officers in certain white vans whose locations students report in support groups.

Risk Levels by Area

  • Established marākiz and major institutes: Generally not targeted — lower risk
  • Certain areas in Cairo: Ask locals and other students which areas to avoid before exploring
  • Villages with Ahl al-Sunnah: Very high risk — see the Islamic Sciences section for the full warning

Protect Yourself — Practical Steps

  1. Carry iqāmah and passport every time you leaveOr a photocopy. When iqāmah is being processed, carry the passport stamp as proof.
  2. Set up an emergency contact in EgyptSomeone who has copies of your documents and can act on your behalf, and contact your family at home.
  3. Send copies homeSomeone at home should have your Egypt contact's details, creating a full communication chain.
  4. Stay informed through support groupsThis is where students report police van locations, raids, and areas to avoid in real time.
  5. Be good to your neighboursGive salām, smile, offer food. Besides being an Islamic obligation, it significantly reduces the chance of a complaint to police.
On Official Discretion

The law's enforcement depends on individual officials. A single officer has significant power to decide on deportation or bans. Respectful conduct and proper documentation at all times are non-negotiable. Cases of wrongful detention despite valid documentation have occurred — being prepared and cautious is the right approach.

Animals

Dogs
Stray dogs are common. Most will not chase if you walk normally. Do not run — it triggers chasing. If approached aggressively, pretend to pick up a rock; this usually deters them. Be more cautious at night.
Cats
Everywhere, will enter buildings freely. Generally scared of people and will not approach. Nothing to be concerned about.
